\begin{abstract}
Capturing and detumbling non-cooperative space debris is a defining challenge for on-orbit
servicing: the target is tumbling, its mass and inertia are unknown, and the chaser is a
\emph{free-floating} platform whose attitude is disturbed by every arm motion. Prior
reinforcement-learning (RL) work for space manipulators concentrates on the pre-contact phase
(reaching and tracking a grasp point), while post-grasp detumbling is handled by model-based
controllers that assume known target dynamics. We address the gap with an RL controller for the
\emph{post-grasp detumbling} phase that is explicitly trained to be \emph{robust to unknown target
inertia} via domain randomization over the target mass, inertia tensor, and initial angular
velocity. We evaluate in a MuJoCo free-floating-base simulation with full contact dynamics against
an impedance / active-resistance baseline, reporting detumbling time, contact force, and induced
base-attitude disturbance. \todo{headline result: learned policy holds success across $0.5\times$--$2\times$
nominal inertia where the model-based baseline degrades by $Y\%$.}
\end{abstract}

\subsection{MDP}
State $s\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$: base pose+twist, joint positions/velocities, target relative
pose and angular velocity, end-effector-to-grasp vector. Action $a\in[-1,1]^3$: normalized joint
torques. Reward penalizes target spin, end-effector distance, induced base angular velocity, and
control effort, with a terminal bonus when the target angular velocity falls below a threshold:
\begin{equation}
r = -w_\omega\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}_t\rVert - w_d d_{\text{eg}}
    - w_b\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}_b\rVert - w_u\lVert a\rVert^2 + r_{\text{term}}.
\end{equation}
