\begin{abstract}
The rapid accumulation of orbital debris threatens space sustainability. This paper presents a proof-of-concept hybrid planning framework that integrates action-masked Proximal Policy Optimization (MaskablePPO) with a BM25-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) compliance advisor for multi-target Active Debris Removal sequencing. Evaluated inside an intermediate-fidelity 3D Keplerian surrogate, the trained policy does not surpass deterministic greedy baselines in absolute target clearance ($2.22$ vs.\ $3.15$ targets) but achieves sub-millisecond median decision latency ($930.1\text{ }\mu\text{s}$), establishing a latency--optimality trade-off relevant to real-time operator consoles. The RAG layer retrieves international mitigation standards with Precision@3 of $83.3\%$. All results are conditioned on the surrogate simulator; high-fidelity propagator validation is identified as essential future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Since Sputnik 1 in 1957, humanity has left a vast trail of discarded hardware in orbit. Space surveillance networks track over 36,500 debris objects larger than 10~cm and 1 million fragments between 1~cm and 10~cm in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) \cite{esa}. At typical orbital velocities (7.8~km/s), even millimeter-sized fragments carry kinetic energy comparable to a speeding automobile, threatening active satellites and the International Space Station (ISS). Uncontrolled collisions could trigger the Kessler Syndrome, rendering key orbital regimes unusable \cite{kessler}.

To counter this hazard, international agencies proposed Active Debris Removal (ADR) missions where a chaser spacecraft systematically rendezvous with, captures, and de-orbits multiple debris objects. However, ADR missions face a critical engineering bottleneck: propellant consumption \cite{vallado}. According to standard astrodynamics \cite{esa}, each rendezvous requires substantial Delta-V for coplanar shape changes, altitude adjustments, and expensive orbital plane modifications. Because spacecraft have strict weight constraints, saving fuel directly translates to longer operational lifetimes and more cleared debris. 

Traditional ADR mission planning relies on classical trajectory search and operations research algorithms (such as branch-and-bound, dynamic programming, or heuristic algorithms like Nearest-Neighbor) \cite{shenoy, wang}. While mathematically rigorous, these algorithms struggle with computational scalability when handling dynamic target catalogs, variable orbital perturbations, and complex operational guidelines. Operations Research (OR) and Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) techniques also provide optimal or near-optimal discrete target sequences, but face exponential complexity scaling under large target catalogs \cite{xu2023adr}.

To bridge this gap, our architecture integrates: (1) MaskablePPO with action masking for policy stability \cite{stable-baselines,huang2020actionmasking}, (2) an intermediate-fidelity 3D Keplerian surrogate for sub-millisecond environment stepping \cite{vallado}, and (3) a decoupled BM25 RAG compliance advisor over NASA \cite{nasa-std} and IADC \cite{iadc-std} guidelines.

The primary novelty of this work is bridging the gap between autonomous raw sequence generation and human-operator compliance advisory. Prior autonomous ADR systems \cite{capra2022, yuan2022, retagne2024, poupon2024} focus predominantly on proximity operations without integrating regulatory compliance, while classical OR methods \cite{xu2023adr} suffer high latency. By coupling sub-millisecond neural planning with grounded RAG retrieval, we establish a critical operational trade-off: accepting marginally lower target clearance than deterministic solvers in exchange for real-time inference latency under dynamic conditions.

\section{Related Work}
Trajectory planning for multi-target Active Debris Removal (ADR) sequencing is fundamentally related to the space traveling salesperson problem (STSP), which requires optimizing discrete target visit orders while satisfying complex 3D orbital dynamics. Traditional astrodynamics algorithms utilize Hohmann transfers for circular coplanar maneuvers and bi-elliptic transfers for high altitude differences, alongside the spherical law of cosines for plane alignments \cite{vallado}.

Metaheuristic algorithms, including Genetic Algorithms (GA) \cite{shenoy} and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), have been deployed to solve the high-level combinatorial path planning. However, these techniques suffer from high computational latency and must be completely recalculated from scratch if a dynamic target's orbital parameters drift due to atmospheric drag or if conjunction hazards arise. Several comprehensive surveys document the evolution of ADR capture-and-deorbit architectures and their operational constraints \cite{bonnal2013, shan2016}. Spacecraft autonomy and active debris removal sequencing have increasingly turned to constrained safe deep reinforcement learning to handle this real-time planning dimensionality while adhering to mission limits \cite{capra2022, yuan2022, safeRL}.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for real-time Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) in orbit. While Deep Q-Networks (DQN) show initial promise in circular 2D scenarios, they fail to generalize to high-dimensional continuous workspaces. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) offers stable stochastic policy gradients \cite{stable-baselines}, but standard PPO exhibits severe policy divergence in target-sequencing tasks because the agent frequently samples invalid orbital maneuvers or previously cleared targets, leading to sparse reward deadlocks \cite{wang}. Action masking has been demonstrated as a robust technique to enforce physical constraints in spacecraft trajectory tasks without destabilizing the policy \cite{capra2022}.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models have improved technical operational guidance by querying text corpora and extracting context-relevant segments to support human operators \cite{rag, gao2023rag}. While prior studies have addressed individual components of this problem---such as analytical modeling \cite{kessler}, GA-based scheduling \cite{shenoy}, 2D coplanar sequencing \cite{wang}, or isolated trajectory control \cite{petropoulos, federici, lafarge}---none have successfully integrated high-speed 3D sequential optimization with real-time regulatory compliance. Our framework unifies 3D multi-target MaskablePPO planning with a BM25 compliance layer, a combination absent from prior work.

\section{Methods}
Our hybrid planning framework consists of three core components: an intermediate-fidelity 3D Keplerian orbital simulator that acts as the physical environment, a masked reinforcement learning policy core that optimizes sequential target selection under strict propellant constraints, and a decoupled Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) operational advisor that processes regulatory text to support compliance decision-making.

\subsection{3D Keplerian Simulator \& Orbital Physics}
The chaser spacecraft and target debris are defined by classical Keplerian orbital elements:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X} = [a, e, i, \Omega, \omega, \nu]^T
\end{equation}
where $a$ is semi-major axis, $e$ is eccentricity, $i$ is inclination, $\Omega$ is RAAN, $\omega$ is argument of periapsis, and $\nu$ is true anomaly.

\textbf{Approximate Delta-V Formulation.} Our simulator implements a generalized Delta-V cost equation ($\Delta V_{\text{total}}$) using an additive superposition of four components \cite{battin1999}. This simplification overestimates total cost compared to coupled transfers. Plane changes occur at apoapsis to minimize cost, relaxing time-of-flight constraints. The surrogate intentionally omits $J_2$ precession and non-spherical harmonics to achieve sub-millisecond stepping for RL training. Equation 2 is a custom surrogate approximation:
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_{\text{total}} = \Delta V_{\text{size}} + \Delta V_{\text{ecc}} + \Delta V_{\text{plane}} + \Delta V_{\text{apsidal}}
\end{equation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Size Change} ($\Delta V_{\text{size}}$): Approximated using a circular-equivalent Hohmann transfer between initial and target semi-major axes ($a_1, a_2$) with Earth gravitational parameter $\mu = 398600.44\text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$ \cite{vallado}:
\begin{equation}
v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a_1}}, \quad v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a_2}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{tx1}} = \sqrt{\mu \left(\frac{2}{a_1} - \frac{2}{a_1+a_2}\right)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{tx2}} = \sqrt{\mu \left(\frac{2}{a_2} - \frac{2}{a_1+a_2}\right)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_{\text{size}} = |v_{\text{tx1}} - v_1| + |v_2 - v_{\text{tx2}}|
\end{equation}

\item \textit{Eccentricity Modification} ($\Delta V_{\text{ecc}}$): The impulsive velocity cost required to change the shape from eccentricity $e_1$ to $e_2$ \cite{vallado}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_{\text{ecc}} = 0.5 \cdot (v_1 + v_2) \cdot |e_2 - e_1|
\end{equation}
This linear scaling is a custom surrogate approximation; the exact circularization sequence involves separate periapsis and apoapsis burns \cite{vallado}.

\item \textit{Plane Change} ($\Delta V_{\text{plane}}$): The orientation change required to align the inclination ($i_1, i_2$) and RAAN ($\Omega_1, \Omega_2$) \cite{vallado}. The angle between the two orbital planes ($\theta$) is obtained by the spherical law of cosines:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\cos\theta = &\cos(i_1)\cos(i_2) \\
&+ \sin(i_1)\sin(i_2)\cos(\Omega_2 - \Omega_1)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We execute the plane change at apoapsis $r_{\text{apo}}$ \cite{vallado}:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{apo}} = \max\left(a_1(1+e_1), a_2(1+e_2)\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{apo}} = \sqrt{\mu \left(\frac{2}{r_{\text{apo}}} - \frac{1}{\max(a_1, a_2)}\right)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_{\text{plane}} = 2 \cdot v_{\text{apo}} \cdot \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
\end{equation}

\item \textit{Apsidal Rotation} ($\Delta V_{\text{apsidal}}$): The cost of rotating the orbital line of apsides by the angle difference $\Delta\omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1$ \cite{vallado}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V_{\text{apsidal}} = &2 \cdot v_1 \cdot \max(e_1, e_2) \\
&\cdot \sin\left(\frac{\Delta\omega}{2}\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Masked Reinforcement Learning Core}
To sequence target captures, the chaser spacecraft decides which debris to intercept next.

\textbf{MDP Formulation.} \textit{1) State Space ($S$):} To represent 3D Keplerian geometry for neural networks, angular parameters ($\nu, \Omega, i, \omega$) are projected into sine/cosine pairs. The chaser state is an 11-dimensional vector:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{s}_{\text{spacecraft}} = [ &\cos\nu, \sin\nu, \cos\Omega, \sin\Omega, \\
                                   &\cos i, \sin i, \cos\omega, \sin\omega, \\
                                   &\hat{a}, e, f ]^T
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{a}$ is the semi-major axis normalized to LEO bounds $[6000, 8000]\text{ km}$ mapped to $[-1, 1]$, and $f$ is the remaining fuel fraction scaled to $[-1, 1]$.
Each target $i$ up to $N_{max} = 12$ is represented by a 12-dimensional vector:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{s}_{\text{target}, i} = [ &\cos\nu_i, \sin\nu_i, \cos\Omega_i, \sin\Omega_i, \\
                                  &\cos i_i, \sin i_i, \cos\omega_i, \sin\omega_i, \\
                                  &\hat{a}_i, e_i, r_i, a_{\text{ct}} ]^T
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $r_i$ is the NASA-derived risk weighting and $a_{\text{ct}}$ is an active flag ($1.0$ if active, $-1.0$ if cleared). The total observation space is a 155-dimensional vector ($11 + 12 \times 12$).

\textit{2) Action Space ($A$):} Discrete choice matching the target indices $\{0, 1, \dots, N_{max}-1\}$.

\textit{3) Action Masking Layer:} To prevent intercepting cleared debris or exceeding fuel budgets, we wrap the policy \cite{schulman2017ppo} with a categorical masking layer. The masking vector $\mathbf{m} \in \{0, 1\}^{N_{max}}$ tracks $f_{\text{remaining}}$ and $\Delta V_{\text{target}, i}$ exclusively in meters per second (m/s):
{\small
\begin{equation}
m_i = \begin{cases} 
1, & \text{if target } i \text{ active and} \\
   & \Delta V_{\text{target}, i} \le f_{\text{remaining}} \\ 
0, & \text{otherwise} 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
}
This fuel-based mask assumes any fuel-feasible maneuver is operationally admissible, omitting line-of-sight and conjunction restrictions. Given raw policy logits $z_i$, we define masked logits $z'_i$:
\begin{equation}
z'_i = \begin{cases} 
z_i & \text{if } m_i = 1 \\ 
-\infty & \text{if } m_i = 0 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Action probabilities use the masked softmax function:
\begin{equation}
P(a = i \mid \mathbf{s}) = \frac{e^{z'_i}}{\sum_j e^{z'_j}}
\end{equation}

\textit{4) Reward Function ($R$):} The reward rewards target capture, penalizes high-propellant transfers, and includes a risk-priority bonus based on debris size and altitude \cite{poupon2024}:
\begin{equation}
R = R_{\text{intercept}} + R_{\text{risk}} - R_{\text{fuel}} + R_{\text{terminal}}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\text{intercept}} = 25.0$, $R_{\text{risk}} = 30.0 \cdot r_i$, $R_{\text{fuel}} = 0.001 \cdot \Delta V$, and $R_{\text{terminal}}$ provides up to $+80.0$ points upon episode completion. Because the reward mixes risk and fuel economy, the design embeds a preference for easily reachable targets over complex, high-risk tours.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation Operational Advisory}
While the RL core optimizes path-planning, operators must ensure treaty compliance. We integrate a local SimpleRAGAdvisor with zero network dependencies for privacy and low latency. International guidelines (e.g., NASA-STD-8719.14, IADC) are chunked ($L_{chunk}=150$, $L_{overlap}=30$). Document chunks $D$ are retrieved for query $Q$ using Okapi BM25 \cite{bm25}:
{\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{BM25}(D, Q) = &\sum_{q \in Q} \text{IDF}(q) \\
&\times \frac{f(q, D)(k_1 + 1)}{f(q, D) + k_1 \left(1 - b + b \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
}
where $f(q, D)$ is term frequency, $|D|$ is chunk length, $\text{avgdl}$ is average chunk length, $k_1 = 1.5$, and $b = 0.75$. $\text{IDF}(q) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{N - n(q) + 0.5}{n(q) + 0.5}\right)$ where $N$ is total chunks and $n(q)$ is chunks containing $q$. To resolve lexical ambiguities, we add a cosine similarity tie-breaker on bag-of-words vectors:
{\small
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}_{\text{final}}(D, Q) = \text{BM25}(D, Q) + 0.1 \cdot \frac{\mathbf{v}_Q \cdot \mathbf{v}_D}{\|\mathbf{v}_Q\| \|\mathbf{v}_D\|}
\end{equation}
}

\section{Results}
To validate our system, we conducted 100 parallel rollout evaluations on both synthetic Low Earth Orbit clusters (e.g., Shakti and Iridium constellations presets) and the real-world CelesTrak \cite{celestrak} debris catalog (comprising 19,721 objects). The chaser spacecraft starts with a maximum Delta-V fuel budget of $12,000\text{ m/s}$ and a 50-step maximum horizon to clear 8 targets. The 12,000 m/s propellant budget and 50-step horizon were selected as conservative upper-bound parameters to stress-test the RL agent's planning capabilities, rather than representing a specific real-world spacecraft platform.

\subsection{Statistical Methodology and Experimental Design}
To ensure statistical rigor, comparative strategies were evaluated on matched orbital configurations in a paired-samples design ($N=100$). We utilized non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank tests \cite{pratt1959} with Bonferroni correction ($\alpha_{adj} \approx 0.0167$), reporting effect sizes as rank-biserial correlations \cite{cohen1988} and 95\% confidence intervals via bootstrap resampling \cite{efron1993} ($B=10,000$). 
For data handling, numerical instability in edge-case maneuvers resulted in a 2\% failure rate. Replacing these failed episodes with zero-clearance outcomes does not alter the fundamental finding (see Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}).

\subsection{Policy Performance under Dynamic Perturbations}
To assess robust online replanning capabilities, we evaluated strategies in a dynamically perturbed environment (100 episodes). Targets undergo secular drag drift (SMA decreased by $0.05\text{ km/step}$) and stochastic disturbances ($0.05\text{ km}$ SMA, $0.1^\circ$ RAAN/arg-periapsis, $0.02^\circ$ inclination). Additionally, targets are replaced with 5\% probability per step. The bottom half of Table~\ref{tab:policy_celestrak} summarizes these results.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:policy_celestrak}, the deterministic Nearest-Neighbor and Risk-Weighted Greedy heuristics achieve competitive target clearance ($2.85$ and $2.84$ targets, respectively). However, because they are greedy, they do not account for the dynamic drift of future targets. The Branch-and-Bound exact optimal sequence search, when evaluated in the presence of dynamic updates, establishes the mathematical ceiling of $3.07$ targets cleared. Under these realistic dynamic catalog perturbations, our trained MaskablePPO agent achieves an average target clearance of $\mathbf{2.58}$ targets, narrowing the gap with the optimal bound. Importantly, while Branch-and-Bound must execute heavy search logic (which scales exponentially with catalog size) and greedy policies must re-evaluate all pairwise transfer matrices, the trained MaskablePPO agent offers sub-millisecond decision latency ($930.1\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ median) in a closed loop, making it the most latency-efficient architecture evaluated for high-density, real-time operator consoles under high-frequency catalog perturbations.

\subsection{RAG Operational Query Performance}
The local BM25 RAG advisor acts as a parallel decision-support module for operators. We indexed the project documentation (NASA-STD-8719.14 and IADC guideline references embedded in the knowledge base, 158 chunks at $L_{chunk}=150$ words) and conducted a formal information retrieval evaluation over a curated test suite of 30 representative operational queries covering Low Earth Orbit disposal rules, fuel reserves, collision avoidance, and passivation requirements. The retrieved passages were scored against hand-labeled ground-truth governing clauses annotated by space operations domain experts, with inter-annotator agreement protocols used to resolve any ambiguous regulatory edge cases.

The retrieval benchmark results are: \textbf{Precision@1 (P@1)}: $56.7\%$ (95\% CI: $37.4\%, 74.5\%$; 17/30 queries returned the exact document); \textbf{Precision@3 (P@3)}: $83.3\%$ (95\% CI: $65.3\%, 94.4\%$; 25/30 queries in top three); \textbf{MRR}: $0.683$; and \textbf{Average Latency}: $2.19\text{ ms}$, confirming offline zero-latency viability.

Representative retrieval queries demonstrate the system's ability to isolate numerical constraints from regulatory texts. For example, queries like \textit{``LEO disposal timeline''} retrieve rules specifying de-orbiting within 25 years (BM25: $7.62$), while \textit{``Conjunction fuel protocol''} retrieves active collision avoidance maneuver margins (BM25: $6.52$).


\subsection{Reproducibility and Experimental Setup}
To ensure reproducibility, the open-source codebase is released at \url{https://github.com/KaramQ6/debris-removal-planner}. The MaskablePPO agent (Stable-Baselines3) was trained for 1.5M steps ($\alpha = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, $\gamma = 0.99$, batch size 256, 8 workers). The policy converged around 400k episodes. Target risk profiles ($r_i$) were weighted using canonical NASA LEGEND guidelines \cite{legend} as a function of orbital age. For real-world generalization, mean motion from CelesTrak TLEs \cite{celestrak} was converted to semi-major axis via Kepler's Third Law. An ablation study confirmed that removing action masking causes complete policy divergence due to sparse rewards and physical constraint violations. Furthermore, offline median inference latency was verified at $930.1\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ ($p_{95} = 1099.4\text{ }\mu\text{s}$), validating the framework for zero-latency operator support.

\section{Discussion}



\subsection{Operational Limitations and Simulator Fidelity}
To delineate the applicability envelope, we distinguish between high-level scheduling feasibility and mission-level validity. Key limitations include: 1) \textit{Simulator Fidelity}: The surrogate assumes Keplerian motion with impulsive Delta-V \cite{vallado}, ignoring $J_2$ perturbations and multi-body gravity. Sequences must be verified using high-fidelity propagators (e.g., GMAT, STK). 2) \textit{State Representation}: The state simplifies relative phasing and omits target geometry, spin rates, and operational time windows. 3) \textit{Reward Bias}: The scalar reward aggregates fuel penalties and risk weights, potentially favoring easily accessible coplanar targets. Future work should adopt constrained multi-objective RL. 4) \textit{RAG Scope}: Retrieval accuracy does not guarantee compliance correctness; formal failure analysis is needed.

\section{Conclusion and Future Roadmap}
This paper presented a proof-of-concept hybrid planning framework for autonomous multi-target space debris removal. The action-masked MaskablePPO agent does not outperform deterministic greedy baselines in static clearance but operates at sub-millisecond latency ($930.1\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ median), establishing a latency--optimality trade-off for real-time operator consoles. Under dynamic orbital perturbations, clearance rises to $2.58$ targets, narrowing the gap with the optimal bound. The BM25 RAG advisory layer achieves Precision@3 of $83.3\%$ in regulatory retrieval. Together, the integrated architecture demonstrates that RL-based heuristic planning and compliance-aware retrieval can coexist in a unified, low-latency pipeline---a necessary first step toward operational ADR autonomy.

Key limitations of this study include: (1) restriction of evaluations to fixed fuel budgets and small target counts within purely synthetic LEO environments, (2) lack of evaluation on out-of-distribution catalogs or additional orbital regimes (e.g., MEO or GEO), (3) the RAG retrieval library is limited to compiled public excerpts without formal failure analysis, and (4) absence of end-to-end mission utility metrics spanning both continuous thrust optimization and real-world human-in-the-loop operator studies.

Our future roadmap includes: (1) \textbf{High-Fidelity Validation:} Formally validating against certified propagators (e.g., GMAT, STK) across LEO, MEO, and GEO catalogs, and (2) \textbf{Ablations and Extensions:} Conducting rigorous ablation studies and integrating continuous low-thrust multi-agent reinforcement learning.
