# IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines — Key Excerpts

> Source: Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC), IADC-2002-01 Rev. 3
> These are publicly available excerpts for educational and research purposes.

## 1. Limitation of Debris Released During Normal Operations

Spacecraft and orbital stages should be designed not to release debris during
normal operations. Where this is not feasible, any release of debris should be
minimized in number, area, and orbital lifetime.

## 2. Minimization of Potential for On-Orbit Break-ups

### 2.1 Minimization of Stored Energy
All on-board sources of stored energy should be depleted when they are no longer
required for mission operations or post-mission disposal. Depletion should occur
as soon as this operation does not pose an unacceptable risk to the payload.

### 2.2 Avoidance of Intentional Destruction
Intentional destruction of a spacecraft or orbital stage (self-destruct,
intentional collision, etc.) that could generate long-lived debris should be
avoided unless the risk to ground safety is unacceptable.

## 3. Post-Mission Disposal

### 3.1 Geosynchronous Region
A spacecraft or orbital stage in or near the geosynchronous region at end of
mission should be maneuvered to an orbit above the GEO protected region.

### 3.2 Low Earth Orbit
Spacecraft or orbital stages that have terminated their operational phase in
orbits that pass through the LEO protected region should be de-orbited or
maneuvered into an orbit with a lifetime short enough to limit the risk to
other spacecraft.

The recommended maximum orbital lifetime after end of mission is **25 years**.

## 4. Prevention of On-Orbit Collisions

Mission planning should include an assessment of the probability of collision
with known objects during the spacecraft's orbital lifetime. If the probability
of collision with a known object is unacceptable, a collision avoidance maneuver
should be planned and executed.

### 4.1 Conjunction Assessment
Operators should screen their spacecraft against the catalog of tracked objects.
When a conjunction is identified with unacceptable collision probability, a
debris avoidance maneuver should be planned with sufficient lead time.

### 4.2 Fuel Reservation
Operators should reserve sufficient propellant for at least **two collision
avoidance maneuvers per year** during the operational phase and for end-of-life
disposal maneuvers.

## 5. Debris Removal Considerations

Active debris removal missions should prioritize objects that:
- Reside in high-density orbital regions
- Have high collision probability with operational assets
- Have large cross-sectional area (higher collision risk per object)
- Cannot de-orbit naturally within 25 years

### 5.1 Removal Mission Fuel Planning
Removal missions should plan fuel budgets that account for:
- Transfer maneuvers between successive targets
- Proximity operations for capture
- De-orbit burns for each captured object
- Reserve margin for unexpected conjunction avoidance

When fuel margin becomes constrained (below 15% of total budget), missions
should prioritize the highest-risk remaining targets and avoid transfers that
consume more than 40% of remaining fuel for a single target.
